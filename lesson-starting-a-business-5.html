Starting a Business · Lesson 5

Pricing, Costs & Basic Operations

A business needs more than a useful offer. It also needs a practical way to price, deliver, track costs, and operate. Understanding these basics helps you make decisions using real numbers instead of guesses.

Learning path progress 5 of 6

Pricing is a business decision

A price is the amount a customer pays for an offer. Setting a price involves more than simply choosing a number that feels attractive.

A useful starting point is to understand the value of the offer, the costs involved in delivering it, the customers being served, and the alternatives available in the market.

Revenue is not the same as profit. Money received from customers may still need to cover product costs, delivery, staff, tools, taxes, fees, refunds, and other business expenses.

Understand your costs

Direct costs Costs directly connected to delivering a particular product or service.
Operating costs Expenses required to keep the business running, such as software, communication, workspace, or administration.
Transaction costs Fees that may occur when collecting payments, transferring money, or using a platform.
Unexpected costs Expenses such as refunds, replacements, repairs, delays, or other situations that require planning.

A simple pricing calculation

Imagine a service is sold for ₦10,000. If ₦6,000 is spent directly on delivering that service, the remaining ₦4,000 is not automatically final profit. Other business expenses may still need to be paid.

This is why businesses should track both money coming in and money going out.

Basic operations

Operations are the practical activities that allow a business to deliver what it promises.

  • Receiving and recording customer orders
  • Delivering products or services
  • Managing inventory or required resources
  • Communicating with customers
  • Recording income and expenses
  • Handling support, corrections, refunds, or complaints
  • Reviewing results and improving the process

Keep business records

Even a small business can benefit from simple records. Track sales, expenses, outstanding payments, refunds, and other important financial activity.

Good records help you understand whether the business is actually generating a surplus, where money is being spent, and which parts of the operation need attention.

Start with simple numbers

You do not need a complicated accounting system on your first day. Start by clearly recording what comes in, what goes out, what is owed, and what belongs to customers or other parties.

As the business grows, the record-keeping system can become more detailed.
