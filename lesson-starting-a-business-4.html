Starting a Business · Lesson 4

Creating Value & Choosing an Offer

A business creates value when it helps people achieve something they care about. Your offer is the specific product, service, or solution you provide to make that value available.

Learning path progress 4 of 6

Start with value

A business is not valuable simply because it has a product or service. The important question is whether the offer provides something useful to the people it is designed for.

Value is the useful outcome a customer receives. The offer is the way the business delivers that value.

For example, a customer may not actually want a particular tool, lesson, delivery service, or piece of software by itself. They may want to save time, learn a skill, solve a problem, complete a task, reduce inconvenience, or achieve another useful outcome.

Product, service, or solution?

Product Something a customer can buy or use, such as a physical item or digital product.
Service Work performed for a customer, such as design, tutoring, repair, consulting, or delivery.
Solution A combination of products, services, or processes designed around a particular customer need.

Make the offer clear

A strong early offer should be understandable. A potential customer should be able to see what you provide, who it is for, and what useful result it is intended to create.

  • Who is the offer for?
  • What problem or need does it address?
  • What exactly does the customer receive?
  • What outcome or benefit can reasonably be expected?
  • What makes the offer useful compared with available alternatives?

Start with a focused offer

New businesses can be tempted to offer everything at once. A focused offer can make it easier to understand customer needs, test demand, deliver consistently, and improve from real feedback.

Starting focused does not mean staying small forever. As you learn more, you can expand your products, services, customer groups, or delivery methods.

Test before you overbuild

You do not always need to build a large product before learning whether people find the idea useful. A simple prototype, sample service, conversation, demonstration, or small pilot can help you collect evidence.

The goal is not to guarantee success. The goal is to learn what works, what does not, and what customers actually value.
