Starting a Business · Lesson 2

Finding a Problem Worth Solving

Strong businesses often begin by noticing a real problem. Instead of starting with “What can I sell?”, start by asking “What do people need help with?”

Learning path progress 2 of 6

Look for real problems

A problem worth solving does not have to be dramatic. It can be a small inconvenience, an expensive process, a lack of access, wasted time, poor communication, or a task people struggle to complete.

Entrepreneurs can discover opportunities by paying attention to what people repeatedly complain about, what takes too much time, what is difficult to access, and what people already spend money trying to solve.

A useful business question is: “Who has this problem, how often do they experience it, and what are they doing about it today?”

A problem is not automatically a business opportunity

Someone may have a problem without wanting to pay for a solution. Before building a business around an idea, you need to understand the people affected and whether the proposed solution would actually be useful to them.

Where problems can come from

  • Your own experience.
  • Problems experienced by friends, families, or communities.
  • Workplaces and industries.
  • Technology and changing habits.
  • Services that are slow, confusing, expensive, or inaccessible.
  • New needs created by changes in society or markets.

Good observation comes before rushing into a solution. Listen, ask questions, research, and look for patterns. The goal is to understand the problem before deciding what to build or sell.
